AlphaPath

Nursery to Class 10, spoken aloud and offline · in development

What it is

AlphaPath starts where a child does — a single pencil stroke — and keeps going to Class 10. The first chapters teach writing, reading and counting. From Class 1 the work is organised by subject, with practice questions and a short test after each chapter. Every instruction is spoken aloud, so a child who cannot read yet can still use it on their own from the first minute, and none of it needs an internet connection.

How it works

  • Tracing. A guide dot leads the child along each stroke. Finishing always earns something; there is no failure state anywhere in the app.
  • A map, not a menu. Lessons sit along a path the child walks. Earlier chapters stay reachable forever — nothing is ever locked away once passed.
  • A starting point that fits. A short, game-like check finds the right chapter to begin at, instead of guessing from a birthday.
  • Subjects, once there are subjects. From Class 1 the path runs through English, Maths and EVS; from Class 6, English, Maths, Science and Social Science. A grown-up chooses CBSE or Cambridge, and the subjects regroup to match the one they picked.
  • Practice and tests. Chapters end in questions — tap-to-answer and a number pad, each marked as it is answered — then a short test. Class 10 has full mock papers.
  • Grown-up settings behind a gate. Reports, daily goals, screen-time limits and language sit behind a simple multiplication question a young child cannot answer.

Features

  • Daily goal and streak — a ring that fills and a run of days. Missing a day never costs anything.
  • Awards cabinet — things the child has earned, which can never be taken away again.
  • Screen-time limits — set by a grown-up, with a gentle “all done for today” rather than a lock-out. A lesson already started always finishes.
  • Practice reminders — a daily note to the parent, never to the child, and only on days they have not already practised.
  • Works offline — the whole curriculum and every spoken line ship inside the app. Practice done with no signal is kept and sent up later.
  • A report for grown-ups — what is solid, what wants another go, and how much has been practised lately.
  • Progress follows the account — sign in on a new phone and the work is already there.
  • Leaderboards only if you ask for them — off until a grown-up turns them on, never offered below age 8, and a child is never shown by name.
  • Multiple children on one account, each with their own progress.
  • Free, with no ads and nothing to buy inside the app.
  • English throughout, with Hindi part-way in on the grown-up screens and more of it on the way.
